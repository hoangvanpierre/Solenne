# Solenne Brand Voice

**Quietly refined. Naturally warm.**

This document is the reference for every word Solenne publishes: storefront, account,
transactional messages, AI assistant and back-office. When copy and this document
disagree, the document wins — unless the copy is describing a legal or technical fact.

---

## 1. Brand voice principles

Solenne sells scented candles made by hand, in small batches, for ordinary rooms.
The voice should sound like the person who made the candle talking to the person who
bought it: calm, specific, unhurried, and glad you are here.

Five principles, in priority order:

1. **Clarity before atmosphere.** A customer must never have to decode a sentence.
   If a line can be shorter without losing meaning, make it shorter.
2. **Warmth without performance.** We are glad to help. We do not gush, bow, or
   thank the customer four times in one paragraph.
3. **Sensory, but factual.** Describe what a candle smells like and how a room feels.
   Never invent a note, an ingredient, a burn time or a benefit.
4. **Quiet confidence.** Premium is conveyed by precision and restraint, not by
   adjectives. Say "hand-poured in small batches", not "a symphony of artisanal
   excellence".
5. **One voice everywhere.** The same person writes the product page, the cart
   message and the password-reset email. Admin is the same person at work: shorter,
   plainer, still courteous.

### What we are

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Refined | Ornate |
| Warm | Familiar to the point of casual |
| Sensory | Poetic for its own sake |
| Effortless | Vague |
| Premium | Distant, ceremonial, or boastful |
| Personal | Over-familiar ("hey bestie") |

### Inspiration, not imitation

ROJA, CHANEL and Louis Vuitton are references for *principles*: restraint, precise
product language, confidence without exclamation marks, and a customer experience that
feels considered. We never borrow their slogans, signature phrases, campaign lines or
proprietary vocabulary. Solenne must be recognisably its own house.

## 2. Tone characteristics

Tone shifts slightly by context; the personality does not.

| Context | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage / editorial | Warm, evocative, short | "Light for the moments that matter." |
| Product copy | Precise, sensory, factual | "Cedar and sage over a dry amber base." |
| Navigation / buttons | Plain and verb-first | "Add to cart", "View order" |
| Forms | Direct and helpful | "Enter a valid email address." |
| Success | Brief and warm | "Your order has been received." |
| Errors | Honest, never blaming | "We couldn't load your orders. Please try again." |
| Account | Personal but efficient | "Your orders, address and details." |
| Admin | Neutral, operational | "Hide review", "Mark as shipped" |
| AI assistant | Consultative, concise | "If you like cedar, start with Cedar & Sage." |

**Never** make an ordinary UI message sound like a campaign. A cart total is not a
brand moment. A 404 is not an invitation to poetry.

## 3. Vietnamese writing rules

Vietnamese is a first-class language, not a translation of the English. Write it as a
modern Vietnamese retailer would speak to a customer in 2026.

### Pronouns

- **Default to no pronoun.** Vietnamese tolerates subjectless sentences well:
  "Đơn hàng đã được tiếp nhận." is better than "Đơn hàng của quý khách đã được tiếp nhận."
- Use **"bạn"** when direct guidance genuinely helps: "Bạn có thể hủy đăng ký bất cứ lúc nào."
- Do **not** put "bạn" in every sentence, and do not stack "bạn … nhé … ạ".
- **Retire "quý khách"** from customer UI. Keep it only where a formal legal document
  requires it. It is the single largest source of distance in the current site.
- Never use "quý vị", "quý danh", "kính mời", "trân trọng kính gửi", "được ghi nhận".

### Sentence shape

- One idea per sentence. Aim for 8–20 words in UI copy.
- Prefer active, neutral constructions: "Chúng tôi sẽ phản hồi trong hai ngày làm việc."
- Drop ceremonial openers: "Kính mời quý khách khám phá bộ sưu tập" → "Khám phá bộ sưu tập".
- Drop repeated "vui lòng". One per screen is enough; for form errors prefer the
  imperative: "Nhập địa chỉ email hợp lệ."

### Vocabulary

| Use | For | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sản phẩm | product UI, orders, admin | tác phẩm (except editorial) |
| Nến | the candle itself | "tác phẩm nến" in UI |
| Mùi hương / hương thơm | fragrance | "hương sắc", "tinh hương" in UI |
| Bộ sưu tập | collections | "tuyển tập" |
| Đơn hàng | orders | "đơn thỉnh" |
| Giỏ hàng | cart | "giỏ nến" |
| Đánh giá | reviews | "cảm nhận" for the feature name |
| Khám phá | discovery CTAs | "dạo bước", "thưởng ngoạn" |
| Thêm vào giỏ | add to cart | "chọn tác phẩm" |
| Xem chi tiết | detail navigation | "chiêm ngưỡng" |
| Giao hàng | shipping | "giao nhận trang trọng" |
| Miễn phí | free shipping | "trân quý miễn phí" |

**"Tác phẩm"** may stay in genuine editorial writing (journal, brand story) where it
means a crafted object. It must never replace "sản phẩm" in commerce or admin.

### Diacritics and typography

- Always write full diacritics. `Đã giao`, not `Da giao`.
- Use Vietnamese quotation marks “…” and an en dash – for ranges.
- Use a non-breaking idea order: subject → action → detail.
- Keep English product names and scent notes as-is (Cedar & Sage, Bergamot).

## 4. English writing rules

- Natural premium retail English. Read it aloud; if it sounds like a press release,
  rewrite it.
- Active voice, short-to-medium sentences, concrete nouns.
- Sentence case for headings and buttons ("Add to cart", not "Add To Cart" in prose;
  CSS may uppercase visually).
- No honorifics. "Dear valued customer" and "We are delighted to inform you" are banned.
- No exclamation marks in transactional copy. One is acceptable in a genuine
  celebration, and we rarely have one.
- No emoji outside of social captions. The assistant never uses emoji.
- Avoid the words *bespoke*, *curated*, *elevated*, *symphony*, *journey*, *indulge*,
  *luxurious*, *artisanal excellence*, *treat yourself* — they are the vocabulary of
  imitation luxury. Use the concrete fact instead.
- Do not translate Vietnamese word-for-word, and do not write English first and
  translate mechanically. Each language is written to sound native.

## 7. Product storytelling guidelines

Product copy answers two questions, in this order:

1. **What does it smell like?** Name the actual notes, in order.
2. **What experience does it create?** One sentence about mood, room or moment.

Rules:

- Use only verified facts from the product record: name, notes, size, burn time,
  ingredients, price. If a field is empty, write around it — never fill the gap.
- Never invent ingredients, origins, production methods, burn times, health or
  therapeutic benefits, or sustainability claims.
- Do not upgrade a specification to sound more premium (do not turn "soy wax" into
  "rare botanical wax").
- Sensory language is welcome but rationed: one strong image per paragraph, not three.
- Length: 2–4 short paragraphs. A customer should finish it.

**Structure that works**

> Cedar & Sage opens with dry cedar and a green sage edge, then settles into amber and
> a trace of smoke. It suits a study or a reading corner in the evening — the kind of
> scent that stays close rather than filling the room.
>
> Soy wax, cotton wick, 220 g. Approximately 50 hours.

**Avoid**

> A symphony of woodland poetry that transports you to an enchanted forest of eternal
> tranquillity.

## 8. UI and transactional writing guidelines

- **Buttons** are verbs: Add to cart · Checkout · Save changes · Send message · Try again.
- **Labels** are nouns: Email address · Shipping address · Order number.
- **Errors** state what happened and what to do, without blame:
  "We couldn't load your orders. Please try again."
  "Nhập địa chỉ email hợp lệ."
- **Success** confirms and stops: "Your order has been received." Do not add a
  paragraph of gratitude.
- **Empty states** explain the state and offer one next step.
- **Loading** copy is short: "Loading…", "Placing order…", "Đang tải…".
- **Confirmation dialogs** name the action and its consequence, then the buttons repeat
  the action ("Hide review", not "Yes").
- **Accessibility labels** are full sentences describing the control:
  "Remove Cedar & Sage from your wishlist".
- **No dark patterns.** No fake urgency, no invented scarcity, no "only 2 left" unless
  stock data says so.

## 9. AI assistant personality

The Solenne assistant is a knowledgeable fragrance consultant, not a salesperson and
not a chatbot character.

- Understand the request, then answer in 2–4 short sentences.
- Ask a follow-up question only when it genuinely narrows the choice (room, mood,
  intensity, budget).
- Recommend at most two products, always from the live catalogue, and explain the
  choice through actual scent notes.
- Explain differences between products plainly ("Cedar & Sage is drier and quieter;
  Vanilla Ember is sweeter and warmer").
- Be warm and confident. Never pushy, never repetitive, never apologetic twice.
- Never invent attributes, availability, delivery dates, discounts or benefits.
- Decline out-of-scope questions in one sentence and redirect to candles or `/contact`.
- No emoji, no exclamation marks, no markdown headings, no "As an AI…".
- The assistant never says "quý khách". It says "bạn", sparingly.

## 11. Rules for future copy additions

1. **Add both locales in the same change.** `messages/en.json` and `messages/vi.json`
   must keep identical key sets and identical ICU placeholders.
2. **Never hardcode user-facing English or Vietnamese in a component.** Use the
   `messages` catalogues, or the bilingual data modules (`lib/constants.ts`,
   `lib/journal.ts`) when the content is data rather than UI.
3. **Reuse existing keys** before inventing a new one. One concept, one key.
4. **Do not delete or rename keys** without checking dynamic references
   (`t(\`prefix.${value}\`)`, status maps, error-code maps).
5. **Preserve placeholders exactly** (`{count}`, `{name}`, `{min}`, `{max}`, ICU
   plurals). A moved brace is a production bug.
6. **Write the Vietnamese natively**, not from the English. If the English sentence
   has no natural Vietnamese equivalent, rewrite both.
7. **Check the screen in both languages**, including the longest string, at mobile width.
8. **Run the gates**: `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm test:unit`,
   `pnpm test:verify`, `pnpm build`.
9. **Database content is not edited here.** Product descriptions, journal records and
   any copy stored in Supabase are revised through an approved content workflow, not by
   bulk SQL.
10. **When in doubt, say less.** The most premium sentence is usually the shortest one
    that is still true.
